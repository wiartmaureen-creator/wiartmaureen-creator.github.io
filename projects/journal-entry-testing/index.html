Journal entry testing: from 4,466 entries to five decisions

Every financial audit includes testing journal entries for signs of fraud and error. I built a repeatable toolkit of 11 SQL tests that scans a full general ledger in under a second, ranks every entry by risk, and turns the findings into decisions for management and fixes to the processes behind them. It is validated against 30 anomalies planted on purpose.

01 · The business problem

Thousands of entries, a handful that matter

Under the auditing standard on fraud (ISA 240), auditors must respond to the risk that management overrides controls, and testing journal entries is a mandatory part of that response. The question is simple: which entries in the ledger look unusual enough to need a closer look?

In practice this work is often done in Excel, one filter at a time. It is slow, hard to document, and nearly impossible to rerun the same way the following year. Thresholds end up scattered across worksheets, and a long list of hits gives no idea of where to start.

Goal: a toolkit that runs every test in one go, keeps all thresholds in one place, turns the hits into a short prioritised review list, and can show that it actually catches what it is meant to catch.

02 · The data

A realistic ledger with known answers

Real general ledgers are confidential, so I generated one: Lumen Supplies SAS, a fictitious French distributor, over fiscal year 2025. A seeded Python script simulates a full year of activity: invoices from the invoicing system, supplier invoices and payments, payroll, loan instalments, and month-end and year-end closing entries, using French chart of accounts numbering with English labels.

Synthetic data has a second advantage: I could plant 30 known anomalies (fictitious revenue, duplicate invoices, split payments, entries keyed after closing, and more) and measure whether the tests find them, much like testing a control with known positives.

journal_lines 11,317 rows

One row per ledger line: entry number, dates, account, debit/credit, who keyed it and when.

accounts 34 rows

Chart of accounts with account type (asset, liability, revenue, expense).

Sales, purchases, bank, payroll and the manual general journal.

French public holidays for 2025 and early 2026.

auditor tables

audit_parameters, test_catalog, review_keywords, test_hits: thresholds, weights and results.

03 · The approach

Five steps, one command

A single Python script runs the whole pipeline. Every test is a standalone, commented .sql file, and no threshold is hard-coded: they all live in one parameter table.

  1. LoadCSV extracts into a typed SQLite schema with constraints.
  2. Test11 SQL tests, each exporting its own result file.
  3. ScoreWeighted tests combine into one risk score per entry.
  4. BenfordFirst-digit analysis of 11,000+ amounts.
  5. ValidateHits compared with the 30 planted cases.

05 · From findings to decisions

What management should do now

A list of flagged entries is not the end product. Once the legitimate hits are cleared, the review leaves five findings. Each one comes with the amount at stake, a decision for management, and a change to the process so that the problem does not come back.

Findings, amounts at stake, decisions and process improvements

Revenue keyed by hand by the CFO in the last days of the year (3 entries, T09) €85,450 Do not sign off until contracts or delivery notes support each sale; reverse whatever is unsupported. Block manual credits to revenue accounts: revenue only comes from the invoicing system.

profit		 Reverse them, or have them formally approved and documented before the accounts are approved. Lock the 2025 period in the ERP on the closing date.
One invoice paid in four transfers just under the €10,000 approval limit (T07) €39,576 Ask the CFO to approve the payment retrospectively and explain why it was split. Apply the approval limit to the total paid to a supplier over a week, not to each transfer.
Supplier invoices posted twice (5 invoices, T03) €10,241 Cancel the duplicates before they are paid and check that none has been paid already. Make the ERP reject an invoice reference already posted for the same supplier.
Credit notes keyed by the sales manager, who has no posting rights (3 entries, T10) €7,530 Confirm the credit notes with the customers and recover any that are not justified. Remove the sales manager's posting access; credit notes need finance approval.

Bottom line for management: €85,450 of year-end revenue and €70,165 of profit added after closing are not supported yet (the two overlap on one €50,000 entry). The accounts should not be signed off until the CFO provides evidence or the entries are reversed.

Smaller points (two unbalanced accruals, entries keyed at weekends or late at night, vague descriptions) go to the chief accountant for an explanation.

06 · Inside the code

Three queries worth a closer look

Each test is a short, commented SQL file that explains why the test matters before showing how it works.

LAG() compares each entry number with the previous one in the same journal. Any jump larger than one is a gap the client must explain.

WITH numbers AS (
    SELECT DISTINCT
        journal_code,
        SUBSTR(entry_number, 1, INSTR(entry_number, '-'))                      AS prefix,
        CAST(SUBSTR(entry_number, INSTR(entry_number, '-') + 1) AS INTEGER)   AS entry_seq
    FROM journal_lines
),
with_previous AS (
    SELECT
        journal_code,
        prefix,
        entry_seq,
        LAG(entry_seq) OVER (PARTITION BY journal_code ORDER BY entry_seq) AS previous_seq
    FROM numbers
)
SELECT
    journal_code,
    prefix || printf('%05d', previous_seq + 1)  AS first_missing_number,
    prefix || printf('%05d', entry_seq - 1)     AS last_missing_number,
    entry_seq - previous_seq - 1                AS missing_count
FROM with_previous
WHERE entry_seq - previous_seq > 1
ORDER BY journal_code, first_missing_number;

Payments just under the 10,000 approval limit. The window functions count how many such payments each supplier received: one is a coincidence, four in four days is a pattern.

WITH params AS (
    SELECT
        CAST(MAX(CASE WHEN name = 'approval_threshold'   THEN value END) AS REAL) AS threshold,
        CAST(MAX(CASE WHEN name = 'near_threshold_floor' THEN value END) AS REAL) AS floor_share
    FROM audit_parameters
)
SELECT
    jl.entry_number,
    jl.entry_date,
    jl.aux_account                                            AS supplier,
    jl.debit                                                  AS payment_amount,
    ROUND(100.0 * jl.debit / p.threshold, 1)                  AS pct_of_threshold,
    COUNT(*) OVER (PARTITION BY jl.aux_account)               AS near_threshold_payments_to_supplier,
    ROUND(SUM(jl.debit) OVER (PARTITION BY jl.aux_account), 2) AS total_paid_in_these_payments,
    jl.created_by,
    jl.description
FROM journal_lines AS jl
CROSS JOIN params AS p
WHERE jl.journal_code = 'BNK'
  AND jl.account_number LIKE '401%'
  AND jl.debit >= p.threshold * p.floor_share
  AND jl.debit <  p.threshold
ORDER BY near_threshold_payments_to_supplier DESC, supplier, jl.entry_date;

Each test has a weight in the catalogue. An entry's score is the sum of the weights of the tests it triggers, so entries that fail several tests rise to the top.

SELECT
    h.entry_number,
    e.journal_code,
    e.entry_date,
    e.created_at,
    e.created_by,
    e.total_debit                                         AS amount,
    e.description,
    SUM(c.weight)                                         AS risk_score,
    COUNT(*)                                              AS tests_triggered,
    GROUP_CONCAT(h.test_id, ', ' ORDER BY h.test_id)      AS tests
FROM test_hits AS h
JOIN test_catalog AS c
  ON c.test_id = h.test_id
JOIN v_entries AS e
  ON e.entry_number = h.entry_number
GROUP BY h.entry_number, e.journal_code, e.entry_date, e.created_at,
         e.created_by, e.total_debit, e.description
ORDER BY risk_score DESC, tests_triggered DESC, amount DESC;

The validation step: for every planted case, did each test designed to catch it actually flag it? This is what turns "the queries run" into "the queries work".

def check_detection(test_results):
    """Compare what each test flagged with the planted answer key."""
    planted = read_csv(VALIDATION_FILE)
    details = []
    for case in planted:
        expected = case["expected_tests"].split(";")
        triggered = sorted(test_id for test_id, result in test_results.items()
                           if case["entry_number"] in result["flagged"])
        details.append({
            "case_id": case["case_id"],
            "entry_number": case["entry_number"],
            "scenario": case["scenario"],
            "story": case["story"],
            "expected_tests": expected,
            "triggered_tests": triggered,
            "detected": all(test_id in triggered for test_id in expected),
        })
