Journal entry testing: from 4,466 entries to five decisions

The question

Every financial audit tests journal entries for fraud and error. Which entries of a company's general ledger deserve a closer look, and what should management do about them?

Objectives
  • Run 11 audit tests (ISA 240) on a full ledger in one pass
  • Keep every threshold in one parameter table, nothing hard-coded
  • Rank entries by risk so the review starts with the worst
  • Prove the tests work against 30 anomalies planted on purpose
Results produced by the code
30 / 30planted anomalies detected
−99%review workload (33 entries)
€85kmanual revenue flagged
0.5 sto rerun the 11 tests

Decision: do not sign off the accounts until the CFO's €85,450 of year-end manual revenue and the €70,165 of profit added after closing are supported, and lock the period in the ERP.

Automated bank reconciliation: 229 lines, 16 decisions

The question

Every month, finance teams tick bank lines against the ledger by hand. How much can a workbook do on its own, and what is left for a human to decide?

Objectives
  • Import the bank's French CSV and the ledger with Power Query
  • Match lines by reference, then by amount and date
  • Produce the reconciliation statement with integrity checks
  • List only the items to explain, each with a proposed action
Results produced by the workbook

€0.00unexplained difference

Decision: recover €810 overpaid to a supplier (two digits swapped), book the €1,284 insurance debit as a 2026 prepaid expense, and ask the bank to keep ERP references in transfer labels.

Management reporting: sales on budget, margin is not

The question

A retail chain's monthly report says "on budget". Are we making the margin we planned, and if not, where should management act first?

Objectives
  • Model daily sales and the monthly budget as a star schema
  • Define every KPI once, as a DAX measure (21 measures)
  • Build three pages managers can explore on their own
  • Turn the findings into a memo with amounts at stake
Results produced by the model
+0.4%net sales vs budget
−€457kgross margin vs budget
−€481kmargin lost to one promotion
−18%Metz store vs budget

Decision: no blanket 22% barbecue discount next summer, a local plan for the Metz store, better online furniture pages to cut a 9% return rate, more space for outdoor lighting.

Fraud detection: a third of the alerts, twice the fraud caught

The question

A payment provider's fraud team drowns in false alerts while half of the fraud gets through. Which payments should it review, so that fraud losses plus review costs are as low as possible?

Objectives
  • Build behavioural features from each customer's past payments only
  • Compare the current rules with two machine learning models
  • Choose the alert threshold in euros, not in model scores
  • Explain what drives the alerts
Results produced by the code
13 / dayalerts instead of 43
87%of fraud amount caught (vs 44%)
33%of alerts are real fraud (vs 5%)
≈ €19ksaved a month

Decision: replace the three rules with the gradient boosting model after a two-week shadow run, set the threshold from the team's capacity, keep a safety-net rule for very large payments.

What drives housing prices in Lille? Real sales, public data

The question

Buyers, banks and compliance teams all ask: is this price in line with the market? An analysis of every recorded flat and house sale in the Lille metropolitan area, 2021–2025.

Objectives
  • Clean the public DVF records into reliable prices per m²
  • Build a quality-adjusted price index with a regression
  • Measure how much location matters
  • Estimate prices and flag sales far from the market
Results produced by the code
76,047sales analysed (real data)
100.4price index 2025 (2021 = 100)
2.2×price per m², Lille vs Roubaix
±21%typical valuation error

Decision support: a cheap first screen for banks (collateral far above market) and compliance teams (purchases far from market value, a classic money-laundering red flag).

How I work

From raw data to a decision

A dashboard is only useful if it changes what someone does. Every project follows the same four steps, and ends with a recommendation and its impact.

  1. 01

    Frame the decision

    Who will use the result, and what will they decide with it? I start from the business question and the KPI that matters.

  2. 02

    Make the data reliable

    Cleaning, reconciliation and controls. My audit background means the numbers are checked before they are shown.

  3. 03

    Analyse and visualise

    SQL, Python and R for the analysis; Power BI and Excel for reports and dashboards people actually use.

  4. 04

    Recommend and improve

    A clear recommendation, its impact in euros or hours, and the process change that stops the problem coming back.
